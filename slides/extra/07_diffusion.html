Diffusion models

imanolgranada@gmail.com

Where we are

A VAE (Extra 3) and a GAN (Extra 4) turn a random vector into a sample in one network pass. VAEs are stable but blurry; GANs are sharp but hard to train, and they drop modes.

A diffusion model takes a different road: destroy the data with noise in many small steps, and learn to undo one small step. Each step is a plain regression, and the samples are sharp and diverse.

Diffusion model: a fixed process that adds Gaussian noise step by step, and a network $\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t)$ trained to predict that noise. Sampling runs the steps backwards from pure noise.

Four parts: A the forward process, B the reverse process and the loss, C scores, ODEs and faster sampling, D guidance, text and latent diffusion. It ends with the Extra 7 lab.

Part A
Destroying data:
the forward process

  • noise, then denoise

  • jump to any step
  • schedules and the SNR

The idea: destroy slowly, rebuild slowly

Forward (fixed): add a little Gaussian noise, 1000 times. At the end the image is gone; only $\Nc(\mathbf 0,\bI)$ is left, whatever the data were.

Reverse (learned): one network removes a little noise per step. Start from pure noise and run it 1000 times: a new sample.

Why small steps? Undoing a small Gaussian step is itself almost Gaussian, so the network only predicts a mean: a regression with a squared error. No adversary as in a GAN, no encoder to learn as in a VAE. The price: many network calls per sample.

Used in Stable Diffusion, DALL·E 2 and 3, Imagen, Midjourney (images); Sora, Veo (video); DiffWave (speech); RFdiffusion and AlphaFold 3 (proteins); GenCast (weather).

One forward step

ᾱt
signal √ᾱt
noise √(1−ᾱt)

Step $t$ shrinks the point a little and adds noise of variance $\beta_t$:

$$q(\bx_t\mid\bx_{t-1})=\Nc\big(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\bx_{t-1},\ \beta_t\bI\big)$$

that is $\ \bx_t=\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\,\bx_{t-1}+\sqrt{\beta_t}\,\beps$, with $\beps\sim\Nc(\mathbf 0,\bI)$.

Why $\sqrt{1-\beta_t}$: if $\bx_{t-1}$ has variance 1, then $\bx_t$ has $(1-\beta_t)\cdot1+\beta_t=1$. The process is variance preserving: the cloud never blows up, it only loses its shape.

DDPM (Ho et al., 2020): $T=1000$, $\beta_t$ from $10^{-4}$ to $0.02$. The first step is $\bx_1=0.99995\,\bx_0+0.01\,\beps$.

Notation: $\alpha_t=1-\beta_t$ and $\abar_t=\alpha_1\alpha_2\cdots\alpha_t$, the signal kept after $t$ steps.

Jumping to any step: the closed form

Induction. Assume $\bx_{t-1}=\sqrt{\abar_{t-1}}\,\bx_0+\sqrt{1-\abar_{t-1}}\,\beps'$. One more step with fresh $\beps''$:

$$\bx_t=\sqrt{\abar_t}\,\bx_0+\underbrace{\sqrt{\alpha_t(1-\abar_{t-1})}\,\beps'+\sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\beps''}_{\text{two independent Gaussians}}$$

Their sum is Gaussian with variance $\alpha_t-\alpha_t\abar_{t-1}+1-\alpha_t=1-\abar_t$. $\square$

$$\bx_t=\sqrt{\abar_t}\,\bx_0+\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps$$

Training never iterates: draw $t$ and $\beps$, jump. Signal power $\abar_t$, noise power $1-\abar_t$: the signal-to-noise ratio $\mathrm{SNR}(t)=\abar_t/(1-\abar_t)$ is the clock of the process.

Noise schedules and the signal-to-noise ratio

Linear (DDPM): $\beta_t$ grows linearly. $\abar_t$ drops below 0.5 at $t=260$, and 33 % of the steps have $\mathrm{SNR}\lt0.01$: almost pure noise, nearly wasted.

Cosine (Nichol and Dhariwal, 2021): $\abar_t=\cos^2\!\big(\tfrac{t/T+s}{1+s}\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)$, SNR 1 at $t=497$, only 6 % of the steps in pure noise. It destroys information more evenly; better for small images.

The forward process on 2-D data

The lab's toy data: three spiral arms, 4000 points, scaled to unit variance like the noise. Every point moves by the same rule, $\bx_t=\sqrt{\abar_t}\,\bx_0+\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps$, with its own $\beps$.

The cloud keeps mean 0 and standard deviation 1 at every step (1.01 and 1.02 at $t=1000$ in the lab): only its shape dissolves.

At $t=1000$ the signal left is $\sqrt{\abar_T}=0.006$: $\bx_T$ is $\Nc(\mathbf 0,\bI)$ for any data. So sampling can start from noise we draw ourselves.

Generation will run this film backwards. The only hard part is the reverse step.

The true reverse step $q(\bx_{t-1}\mid\bx_t)$ needs the distribution of the data: intractable. Given $\bx_0$ it is a product of two Gaussians in $\bx_{t-1}$, $q(\bx_t\mid\bx_{t-1})\,q(\bx_{t-1}\mid\bx_0)$:

$$q(\bx_{t-1}\mid\bx_t,\bx_0)=\Nc\big(\tmu_t,\ \tilde\beta_t\bI\big)$$
$$\tmu_t=\frac{\sqrt{\abar_{t-1}}\,\beta_t}{1-\abar_t}\,\bx_0+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,(1-\abar_{t-1})}{1-\abar_t}\,\bx_t,\qquad \tilde\beta_t=\frac{1-\abar_{t-1}}{1-\abar_t}\,\beta_t$$

Proof: precisions add, $\frac{\alpha_t}{\beta_t}+\frac{1}{1-\abar_{t-1}}=\frac{1}{\tilde\beta_t}$; the mean is the precision-weighted average. The lab checks it by Monte Carlo: 1.6070 ± 0.0448 against 1.6076 ± 0.0451.

For small $\beta_t$ the step without $\bx_0$ is close to a Gaussian too (Feller, 1949). So the model is $p_\theta(\bx_{t-1}\mid\bx_t)=\Nc(\bmu_\theta(\bx_t,t),\sigma_t^2\bI)$: only the mean is learned.

The loss is an ELBO, one KL term per step

As in a VAE (Extra 3): the latents are $\bx_1,\dots,\bx_T$ and the "encoder" $q$ is fixed. The bound splits into one term per step:

$$-\log p_\theta(\bx_0)\le\E_q\Big[L_T+\sum_{t=2}^{T}L_{t-1}+L_0\Big]$$

$L_{t-1}$ compares two Gaussians with the same covariance $\sigma_t^2\bI$, so the KL is a distance between their means:

$$L_{t-1}=\frac{1}{2\sigma_t^2}\,\big\|\tmu_t(\bx_t,\bx_0)-\bmu_\theta(\bx_t,t)\big\|^2$$

$L_T$ has no parameters ($\bx_T$ is noise). A diffusion model is a VAE with 1000 latent layers, a fixed encoder and one shared decoder.

Predict the noise

$$\bx_0=\frac{\bx_t-\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps}{\sqrt{\abar_t}}$$

invert the closed form

$$\tmu_t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(\bx_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\abar_t}}\,\beps\Big)$$

substitute in $\tmu_t$: only $\beps$ is unknown

$$\bmu_\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(\bx_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\abar_t}}\,\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t)\Big)$$

so give the network the same form

$$L_{t-1}=\lambda_t\,\|\beps-\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t)\|^2$$

$\bx_t$ cancels; $\lambda_t=\frac{\beta_t^2}{2\sigma_t^2\alpha_t(1-\abar_t)}$

$$L_{\text{simple}}=\E_{t,\bx_0,\beps}\big\|\beps-\beps_\theta(\sqrt{\abar_t}\,\bx_0+\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps,\ t)\big\|^2$$

Ho et al. (2020) drop $\lambda_t$: the ELBO puts 59 times more weight on $t=2$ than on $t=1000$. $L_{\text{simple}}$ up-weights the noisy steps, where the global structure is decided: better samples, slightly worse likelihood.

The whole loss: noise an example, ask the network for the noise, squared error.

The denoising loss with numbers

The trained toy model of the lab, on a held-out point. Green: $\bx_0$; hollow: $\sqrt{\abar_t}\,\bx_0$; orange arrow: the true noise $\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps$; black: $\bx_t$.

The network sees only $\bx_t$ and $t$. Blue dashed: its noise $\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps_\theta$, ending at $\bx_t$. Star: the implied clean point $\hx_0=(\bx_t-\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps_\theta)/\sqrt{\abar_t}$.

Small $t$: large loss, tiny error on $\hx_0$. Large $t$: loss near 0, $\hx_0$ is a guess. In the lab, held-out loss 0.72 for $t\lt51$ and $\lt10^{-3}$ for $t\gt950$; error of $\hx_0$ 0.007 and 1.28 (per coordinate).

The two are tied: $\|\bx_0-\hx_0\|^2=\|\beps-\beps_\theta\|^2/\mathrm{SNR}(t)$. The training loss plateaus at 0.234, not 0: the same $\bx_t$ can come from many $(\bx_0,\beps)$.

Training and sampling (Ho et al., 2020)

ALGORITHM 1 · TRAINING

repeat

$\quad\bx_0\sim$ data, $\ t\sim\mathcal U\{1,\dots,T\}$, $\ \beps\sim\Nc(\mathbf 0,\bI)$

$\quad\bx_t=\sqrt{\abar_t}\,\bx_0+\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\beps$

$\quad$gradient step on $\ \|\beps-\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t)\|^2$

until converged

One random $t$ per example: every step of the chain is trained, in any order, with one network.

ALGORITHM 2 · SAMPLING

$\bx_T\sim\Nc(\mathbf 0,\bI)$

for $t=T,\dots,1$:

$\quad\mathbf z\sim\Nc(\mathbf 0,\bI)$ if $t\gt1$, else $\mathbf z=\mathbf 0$

$$\bx_{t-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(\bx_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\abar_t}}\,\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t)\Big)+\sigma_t\mathbf z$$

return $\bx_0$

$\sigma_t^2=\beta_t$ or $\tilde\beta_t$. Ancestral sampling: 1000 network calls per sample.

Each sampling step removes a bit of the predicted noise and adds a bit of fresh noise. In the lab the toy model trains in 8000 steps (under a minute on a CPU), and 2000 samples take about 5 s.

The reverse process on 2-D data

The lab's toy network: a residual MLP, 120,450 parameters, with the time embedding added in every block. Algorithm 2, 1000 steps.

As good as real data. Mean distance from a sample to the nearest held-out point: 0.0213; for fresh real points: 0.0217. From the data to the samples: 0.0224 against 0.0219.

No mode dropped. 34.6 %, 32.4 % and 33.1 % of the samples on the three arms (a third each in the data).

The trajectories are random walks: fresh noise at every step. Part C removes that noise.

In code

T = 1000
beta  = torch.linspace(1e-4, 0.02, T + 1); beta[0] = 0
alpha = 1 - beta
abar  = torch.cumprod(alpha, 0)        # abar[t], abar[0] = 1

for x0 in loader:                       # Algorithm 1
    t   = torch.randint(1, T + 1, (len(x0),))
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    a   = abar[t].view(-1, *[1] * (x0.dim() - 1))
    xt  = a.sqrt() * x0 + (1 - a).sqrt() * eps
    loss = F.mse_loss(model(xt, t), eps)
    opt.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); opt.step()

@torch.no_grad()                        # Algorithm 2
def sample(model, shape):
    x = torch.randn(shape)
    for t in range(T, 0, -1):
        tt  = torch.full((shape[0],), t)
        eps = model(x, tt)
        x = (x - beta[t] / (1 - abar[t]).sqrt() * eps) / alpha[t].sqrt()
        if t > 1:
            var = (1 - abar[t-1]) / (1 - abar[t]) * beta[t]
            x = x + var.sqrt() * torch.randn(shape)
    return x

The model takes $\bx_t$ and $t$ and returns a tensor of the shape of $\bx_t$: an MLP for points, a U-Net for images (Part D).

No encoder, no discriminator, no KL term: one regression loss. Training is as stable as fitting any network with MSE.

Everything else is the schedule: three arrays, computed once.

Libraries: Hugging Face diffusers (DDPMScheduler, DDIMScheduler, UNet2DModel) packages exactly these pieces. The lab writes them from scratch.

Denoising = estimating the score

The score of a density, $\nabla_{\bx}\log p(\bx)$, points towards higher density. Let $p_t$ be the distribution of $\bx_t$.

Tweedie's formula: $\ \nabla\log p_t(\bx_t)=-\,\E[\beps\mid\bx_t]/\sqrt{1-\abar_t}$.

Proof: $\nabla_{\bx}q(\bx\mid\bx_0)=-q(\bx\mid\bx_0)\,\beps/\sqrt{1-\abar_t}$; integrate against $p(\bx_0)$ and divide by $p_t(\bx)$: the weights are the posterior $p(\bx_0\mid\bx)$.

The MSE minimiser is $\E[\beps\mid\bx_t]$. So a trained denoiser is a score estimator:

$$\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t)\approx-\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\;\nabla_{\bx}\log p_t(\bx_t)$$

Lab: against the exact score of the training set, median cosine 0.991 at $t=20$, 0.999 at $t=100$. Diffusion and score-based models (Song and Ermon, 2019) are one family.

Langevin dynamics: sampling with a score

With the score of $p$ we can sample $p$ directly. Repeat:

$$\bx\leftarrow\bx+\frac{\eta}{2}\,\nabla_{\bx}\log p(\bx)+\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf z$$

Climb the log-density, plus noise so the points do not collapse onto the modes. For small $\eta$ and many steps, the points are distributed as $p$.

Trap: with separated modes each point stays in the basin where it started, so the weights come out wrong. Annealing starts from a heavily noised $p$ (one blob) and lowers the noise: that is exactly the diffusion chain.

Lab: annealed Langevin with the toy network, 7 noise levels × 30 steps = 210 calls, also draws the spiral (0.0363 / 0.0238, a bit worse than DDPM).

The SDE and ODE view (Song et al., 2021)

With infinitely many tiny steps the forward chain becomes a stochastic differential equation:

$$d\bx=-\tfrac12\beta(t)\,\bx\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\mathbf w$$

It can be run backwards in time, if we know the score:

$$d\bx=\big[-\tfrac12\beta(t)\,\bx-\beta(t)\nabla\log p_t(\bx)\big]dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\bar{\mathbf w}$$

A deterministic ODE has the same marginals $p_t$ at every $t$:

$$\frac{d\bx}{dt}=-\tfrac12\beta(t)\big[\bx+\nabla\log p_t(\bx)\big]$$

DDPM is a discretised reverse SDE; DDIM a discretised ODE. The figure uses the exact score of a 1-D mixture. ODE solvers bring sampling down to 10–50 steps; the ODE also gives exact likelihoods.

DDIM: fewer steps, no noise

Jump from step $t$ to any earlier $t'$ with the same trained network (Song, Meng and Ermon, 2021): guess $\hx_0$, then noise it again to level $t'$:

$$\bx_{t'}=\sqrt{\abar_{t'}}\,\hx_0+\sqrt{1-\abar_{t'}-\sigma^2}\,\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t)+\sigma\mathbf z$$

$\sigma=\eta\sqrt{\frac{1-\abar_{t'}}{1-\abar_t}\big(1-\frac{\abar_t}{\abar_{t'}}\big)}$. $\ \eta=1$, $t'=t-1$: exactly Algorithm 2 (the lab: difference $2\cdot10^{-5}$).

$\eta=0$: DDIM, deterministic. The same $\bx_T$ always gives the same $\bx_0$, so it can be inverted: the lab encodes held-out points to Gaussian latents and decodes them back with mean error 0.021. Basis of image editing.

With 20 steps both are close to 1000 (toy: DDPM 0.0210, DDIM 0.0253, real 0.0217). On large images DDIM wins at 10–50 steps; on our small MNIST model both look alike.

Classifier-free guidance

Train $\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t,c)$ with the label $c$ replaced by "no label" $\varnothing$ 20 % of the time: one network, conditional and unconditional. Sample with

$$\tilde\beps=(1+w)\,\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t,c)-w\,\beps_\theta(\bx_t,t,\varnothing)$$

In scores, with Bayes: $\ \nabla\log p_t(\bx\mid c)+w\,\nabla\log p_t(c\mid\bx)$. An implicit classifier pushes the samples to where $c$ is unmistakable.

Lab, arm 0: mean radius 1.29, 1.48, 1.63, 1.73 for $w$ = 0, 1, 3, 8 (the arm's data: 1.34); distance from the arm's data 0.018 → 0.043. Guidance trades diversity for typicality.

Ho and Salimans (2021). Stable Diffusion writes $s=1+w$, default 7.5. Two network calls per step.

Text conditioning: cross-attention

A frozen text encoder turns the prompt into one vector per token: CLIP in Stable Diffusion (77 tokens × 768 numbers), T5 in Imagen.

Inside the U-Net, cross-attention layers (chapter 6 of the neural networks course): the queries come from the image features, the keys and values from the tokens.

$$\mathrm{softmax}\Big(\frac{\bQ_{\text{image}}\bK_{\text{text}}^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\Big)\bV_{\text{text}}$$

Every pixel takes what it needs from the words. The attention maps show where each word is painted, and editing them edits the image (prompt-to-prompt).

For training, the text is dropped 10 % of the time: classifier-free guidance with a prompt.

Latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022)

Two stages. First an autoencoder ($E$, $D$), as in Extra 3: a small KL term on the latent, plus perceptual and adversarial losses for sharp decodings. Trained once.

Then diffusion on $\bz=E(\bx)$, a 64 × 64 × 4 tensor: 48 times fewer numbers than the 512 × 512 × 3 image, and the U-Net no longer spends its capacity on invisible pixel details.

Stable Diffusion 1: a VAE of 84 M parameters, a U-Net of 860 M, a CLIP text encoder of 123 M; about 150,000 A100 hours of training. Generation runs on a laptop GPU.

The alternative: a cascade in pixel space. Imagen and DALL·E 2 generate a 64 × 64 image, then two diffusion models conditioned on it upsample it to 256 × 256 and 1024 × 1024. Latent diffusion won in practice: one model, and the autoencoder is reused for every task (inpainting, super-resolution, video).

The denoiser: a U-Net with a time embedding

U-Net (as in segmentation): down (strided convolutions), up (transposed convolutions), and skip connections that carry the fine details across. Output: one noise value per pixel.

Time: the step $t$ becomes sines and cosines, as transformer positions, $\big(\sin(\omega_k t),\cos(\omega_k t)\big)$ with $\omega_k=10000^{-k/16}$; an MLP; added to every block as a bias per channel.

Sizes: the lab's U-Net, 590,049 parameters (16 × 16 MNIST); DDPM on CIFAR-10, 35.7 M; Stable Diffusion, 860 M, with self-attention at low resolution. Newer models (SD 3, Sora) replace the U-Net by a transformer on patches.

Cost: one call per step. Lab: 64 digits take 15.5 s with 1000 steps, 0.8 s with 50 (DDIM). Distillation and consistency models go down to 1–4 steps.
