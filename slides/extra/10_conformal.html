Uncertainty and conformal prediction

imanolgranada@gmail.com

Where we are

Every model of the course returns one number per input: a price, a probability, a class. Decisions also need to know how wrong that number can be: the reserve, the price loading, whether to send an expert.

Models can express uncertainty: predict a distribution or quantiles, disagree with themselves (ensembles), give calibrated probabilities. Useful, but none of it comes with a guarantee.

Conformal prediction: from a held-out calibration set, turn any model into intervals or sets $C(x)$ with $P(Y\in C(X))\ge1-\alpha$, in finite samples, assuming only exchangeable data.

Four parts: A uncertainty in models, B split conformal and its guarantee, C adaptive intervals and prediction sets, D practice: shift, time series, communication. It ends with the Extra 10 lab.

Two kinds of uncertainty

Aleatoric: the noise of $Y$ given $X$. Two identical policyholders still have different claims. More data does not reduce it. Here it grows with $x$: heteroscedastic.

Lab data: $Y=f(X)+\sigma(X)\varepsilon$, $\ \sigma(x)=0.1+0.25x$, so the ideal 90% interval is $f(x)\pm1.645\,\sigma(x)$.

Epistemic: what the model does not know. Few data, unseen regions, a wrong model class. More data reduces it.

Prediction intervals need both. Epistemic is the one that warns you when an input is unlike the training data.

Prediction interval vs confidence interval

Confidence interval for the mean $f(x)$: where is the curve? Epistemic only; shrinks like $1/\sqrt n$.

Prediction interval for a new $Y$: where will the next observation fall? It contains the noise too:

$$\Var\big(Y_{\text{new}}-\hat f(x)\big)=\sigma^2(x)+\Var\big(\hat f(x)\big)$$

Lab, bootstrap of the fit: mean CI width $0.552\to0.182$ (ratio 3.03, $\sqrt{10}=3.16$). The PI stays $\approx2.76$ wide.

Same width everywhere: it covers 0.900 overall but 0.989 for $x\lt2.5$ and 0.812 for $x\ge2.5$. A right average, wrong almost everywhere.

Price a portfolio with the CI of the mean; reserve one claim with the PI.

Predicting a distribution

Two heads, $\hmu(x)$ and $\log\hsig^2(x)$, trained by maximum likelihood:

$$\ell=\tfrac12\log\hsig^2(x)+\frac{\big(y-\hmu(x)\big)^2}{2\hsig^2(x)}+\text{const}$$

Constant $\hsig$: the MSE. Free $v=\hsig^2$, one point: $\ \partial\ell/\partial v=\frac1{2v}-\frac{r^2}{2v^2}=0\Rightarrow v=r^2$. The variance head learns the expected squared residual, and noisy points weigh less in the mean.

Log-variance keeps $\hsig^2\gt0$; fit the mean alone (MSE) for the first steps, or the network inflates $\hsig$ to explain its early errors.

Lab: $\hsig/\sigma$ between 0.83 and 1.19; $\hmu\pm1.645\,\hsig$ covers 0.896 (0.903 and 0.888 in the two halves).

The catch: "±1.645 sd" holds 90% only if the noise really is Gaussian.

Quantile regression: the pinball loss

$$\rho_\tau(u)=\max\big(\tau u,\ (\tau-1)u\big),\quad u=y-q$$

Proof that the minimiser is the $\tau$-quantile. For $Y$ with CDF $F$:

$$\frac{d}{dq}\E\,\rho_\tau(Y-q)=-\tau\big(1-F(q)\big)+(1-\tau)F(q)=F(q)-\tau$$

Increasing in $q$ (convex), zero where $F(q)=\tau$. $\ \tau=0.5$: $|u|/2$, the median.

Train two outputs at $\tau=0.05$ and $0.95$: a 90% band, no Gaussian assumption.

Lab: the quantile MLP covers 0.881 on new points, gradient boosting 0.856, the MLP 0.900 on its own training points. Estimated quantiles are noisy: no guarantee.

Epistemic: deep ensembles and MC dropout

Deep ensemble: $M=5$ networks of the previous slide, 5 random seeds. The mixture's variance (law of total variance):

$$\Var(Y\mid x)\approx\underbrace{\tfrac1M\textstyle\sum_m\hsig_m^2}_{\text{aleatoric}}+\underbrace{\tfrac1M\textstyle\sum_m(\hmu_m-\bar\mu)^2}_{\text{epistemic}}$$

MC dropout: keep dropout on at prediction time; 200 passes, same split. One training run instead of $M$.

Lab: epistemic sd ≤ 0.046 inside $[0,5]$, 0.423 at $x=-1.5$, only 0.105 at $x=6.5$ where the tanh networks extrapolate alike.

MC dropout: 0.138 inside, 0.203 at $x=-1.5$. Cheaper, a weaker alarm. Both are heuristics: no guarantee.

Bayesian linear regression in one slide

Prior $\mathbf w\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\tau^2\bI)$, noise $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$, features $\boldsymbol\phi(x)=(1,x)$. The posterior is Gaussian, in closed form:

$$\mathbf S=\Big(\frac{\bI}{\tau^2}+\frac{\Phi^\top\Phi}{\sigma^2}\Big)^{-1},\qquad \mathbf m=\frac{\mathbf S\,\Phi^\top\by}{\sigma^2}$$
$$\Var(y_*\mid x_*)=\underbrace{\sigma^2}_{\text{aleatoric}}+\underbrace{\boldsymbol\phi_*^\top\mathbf S\,\boldsymbol\phi_*}_{\text{epistemic}}$$

The epistemic term shrinks with $n$ and grows away from the data (here the points are in $[-1,0.3]$).

Exact only for linear-Gaussian models. For networks: Laplace, variational inference, or ensembles as a practical approximation. All of it is right only if the prior and the likelihood are.

Calibration and temperature scaling

Calibrated: of the images predicted with confidence 0.8, 80% are right. 15 bins $B_b$ of the top-label confidence:

$$\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_b\frac{|B_b|}{N}\,\big|\mathrm{acc}(B_b)-\mathrm{conf}(B_b)\big|$$

Temperature scaling: $\hat{\mathbf p}=\mathrm{softmax}(\bz/T)$, one scalar $T$ fitted by minimising the NLL on validation data. The order of the logits is kept: same predictions, same accuracy.

Lab, MLP on Fashion-MNIST, 30 epochs: accuracy 0.890, mean confidence 0.927. $T=1.59$: ECE 0.0368 → 0.0155, test NLL 0.352 → 0.315.

A calibrated probability is right on average over many inputs. It still does not say which classes to keep for this image with a guaranteed error rate.

Exchangeability: the only assumption

$(X_1,Y_1),\dots,(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})$ are exchangeable if their joint distribution does not change when we permute them. i.i.d. data are exchangeable.

Fit the model on other data. Then the scores $s_i=s(X_i,Y_i)$ of the $n$ calibration points and of the test point are exchangeable too: no score knows it is special.

So the test score is equally likely to land at any of the $n+1$ ranks. That single fact gives the whole guarantee.

Not exchangeable: time series with drift, a test population unlike the calibration one, calibration data that the model was trained on.

Nothing is assumed about the model, the noise or the dimension of $x$: the model can be a deep network, a GBM or a constant.

Split conformal, step by step

  1. fit $\hmu$ on the training set
  2. scores on $\Dc$: $\ s_i=|y_i-\hmu(x_i)|$
  3. $\hq=$ the $k$-th smallest, $k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$
  4. $C(x)=[\hmu(x)-\hq,\ \hmu(x)+\hq]$

The lab's network $\hmu$ (Part A), fitted on 1,000 training points. Conformal never retrains it.

The first 10 of the 1,000 calibration points. Their $x$ are spread over $[0,5]$; their $y$ are new noise.

Scores sorted: 0.092, 0.102, 0.274, 0.334, 0.345, 0.345, 0.501, 1.044, 1.350, 2.055.

Rank 10 is the point at $x=4.57$, $y=0.12$, far below $\hmu=2.17$: the noisy region.

$n=10$, $\alpha=0.1$: $k=\lceil11\times0.9\rceil=\lceil9.9\rceil=10$. $\ \hq=s_{(10)}=2.055$, the largest score.

Rank 10 of 11: covered unless the test point is the most extreme of the 11, so $P=10/11=0.909$.

$\hmu(2)=1.332$, so $C(2)=1.332\pm2.055=[-0.722,\ 3.387]$.

With all 1,000 points: $k=901$, $\hq=1.397$, coverage 0.901 on 19,000 test points.

Why $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$

The test point is one of $n+1$ exchangeable points, so the slot it may take is counted: $k$ of the $n+1$ ranks must be "covered".

$$\hq=s_{(k)},\quad k=\big\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\big\rceil$$

Equivalently the empirical quantile of the scores at level $(1-\alpha)(1+\frac1n)$. If $k\gt n$, $\hq=+\infty$.

A finite interval needs $n\ge\frac1\alpha-1$: 9 points for 90%, 19 for 95%, 99 for 99%.

The plain 90% quantile of 10 scores (the 9th) covers only $9/11=0.818$. For $n=1000$ the correction is tiny ($k=901$ vs 900): use it always.

The coverage guarantee

THEOREM (Vovk et al., 2005; Lei et al., 2018)

If the $n$ calibration points and the test point are exchangeable,

$$1-\alpha\ \le\ P\big(Y_{n+1}\in C(X_{n+1})\big)\ \le\ 1-\alpha+\frac1{n+1}$$

the upper bound when the scores have no ties.

The probability is over the calibration set and the test point: an average over many deployments, not a promise for each one (next slide).

Ties (discrete scores) only make the rank non-uniform in the safe direction: the lower bound still holds.

Proof (rank argument).

$$Y_{n+1}\in C\iff s_{n+1}\le\hq=s_{(k)}$$

definition of the interval

$$\iff \mathrm{rank}(s_{n+1})\le k$$

among the $n+1$ scores

$$P=\frac{k}{n+1}$$

rank uniform on $1,\dots,n+1$

$$\frac{\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil}{n+1}\in\Big[1-\alpha,\,1-\alpha+\tfrac1{n+1}\Big)$$

since $z\le\lceil z\rceil\lt z+1$

The form of the score never entered the proof: any score works, and Part C uses that freedom.

Coverage varies from one calibration set to another

For one calibration set, the coverage on new points is $c=F_S(\hq)$. The $F_S(s_i)$ are uniform and $\hq=s_{(k)}$, so $c$ is the $k$-th of $n$ uniforms:

$$c\sim\mathrm{Beta}(k,\,n+1-k),\quad \mathrm{sd}\approx\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)/n}$$

Lab, 1,000 random splits: mean coverage 0.9000 for $n=200$ (theory 0.9005); sd 0.063 for $n=20$, with 16.6% of the splits below 0.85; sd 0.0103 for $n=1000$.

Small $n$ also gives wider intervals: mean width 3.12 for $n=20$, 2.79 for $n=1000$.

Rule of thumb: 500 to 1,000 calibration points. The widget simulates the Beta in your browser.

Marginal, not conditional

The theorem is about $P(Y\in C(X))$, averaged over $X$: marginal coverage. We would like it at every $x$:

$$P\big(Y\in C(X)\mid X=x\big)\ge1-\alpha\quad\text{for all }x$$

The band $\hmu\pm1.397$ covers 0.901 overall, made of 1.00 where the noise is small and 0.71 where it is large. The noisy customers get the bad intervals.

Exact conditional coverage is impossible in finite samples without assumptions (Vovk, 2012; Barber et al., 2021). The score decides how close we get.

Better scores: normalised and CQR

Normalised: divide by the predicted spread,

$$s=\frac{|y-\hmu(x)|}{\hsig(x)}\ \Rightarrow\ C(x)=\hmu(x)\pm\hq\,\hsig(x)$$

CQR (Romano et al., 2019): how far $y$ falls outside a quantile band,

$$s=\max\big(\hq_{\alpha/2}(x)-y,\ y-\hq_{1-\alpha/2}(x)\big)$$

$C(x)=[\hq_{\alpha/2}(x)-\hq,\ \hq_{1-\alpha/2}(x)+\hq]$. Same guarantee: the proof never used the score.

A real dataset: California housing

20,640 districts, 8 features, target the median house value (in \$100k). 10,000 train, 5,000 calibrate, 5,640 test. Histogram gradient boosting for the mean and for the 5% and 95% quantiles.

Split conformal: $\pm0.704$ (±\$70k) for every district. Coverage 0.905: 0.939 in the poorest fifth, 0.858 in the richest.

CQR: coverage 0.911, between 0.902 and 0.927 in every quintile, and narrower on average: 1.367 against 1.407.

The raw quantile band, without conformal: only 0.784.

Same guarantee, better intervals: the modelling effort goes into the score.

Prediction sets for classification

Same recipe, a set $C(x)\subseteq\{1,\dots,K\}$ with $P(Y\in C(X))\ge1-\alpha$:

LAC (threshold): $\ s=1-\hat p_y(x)$, so $C(x)=\{k:\hat p_k(x)\ge1-\hq\}$. Smallest sets on average.

APS: sort the classes, add them until the cumulative mass reaches $\hq$. Score = the mass down to the true class, randomised:

$$s=\textstyle\sum_{j:\,\hat p_j\gt\hat p_y}\hat p_j+U\,\hat p_y,\quad U\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$$

Lab, 95% ($\alpha=0.05$; the top-1 is right 0.888 of the time): LAC covers 0.949, threshold $1-\hq=0.203$; APS covers 0.946, $\hq=0.947$.

Without $U$, a confident correct image scores ≈ 1 and the sets explode.

Set size is uncertainty; Mondrian for each class

LAC: 82% singletons, mean size 1.19. APS: mean 1.49. Large sets fall on ambiguous tops; the frequent pairs are Shirt / T-shirt (230) and Coat / Pullover (177).

LAC covers Shirt only 0.853 but Trouser 0.976: a threshold is easy to pass for easy classes. APS spreads it: 0.924 to 0.966.

Mondrian (class-conditional): one $\hq_c$ per class, from that class's calibration images. Exchangeable within a class, so $P(Y\in C\mid Y=c)\ge1-\alpha$ for every $c$.

Lab: the shirt threshold drops to 0.052; mean size 1.19 → 1.32. Averaged over 200 splits every class gets 0.948–0.953. Needs ≥ 19 calibration images per class at 95%.

When exchangeability breaks

Covariate shift

Only the mix of $x$ changes (here: the noisy end). Constant band 0.769; normalised 0.902, CQR 0.872 survive: right at every $x$ ⇒ right for any mix.

Concept shift

$P(Y\mid X)$ changes: 1.5 × the noise. All three fall to 0.75–0.79. Images with pixel noise 0.4: accuracy 0.408, "95%" sets cover 0.474.

What to do

Monitor coverage on fresh labels; recalibrate (cheap: no retraining). Known covariate shift: weighted conformal, scores reweighted by $p_{\text{test}}(x)/p_{\text{cal}}(x)$.

Time series: adaptive conformal inference

A stream whose noise doubles between $t=1000$ and $1500$. ACI (Gibbs and Candès, 2021): recalibrate on a window and adapt the level, with $\mathrm{err}_t=\ind\{y_t\notin C_t\}$:

$$\alpha_{t+1}=\alpha_t+\gamma\,(\alpha-\mathrm{err}_t)$$

A miss widens the next intervals, a hit narrows them a little. For any sequence:

$$\Big|\frac1T\sum_t\mathrm{err}_t-\alpha\Big|\le\frac{\max(\alpha_1,1-\alpha_1)+\gamma}{\gamma\,T}$$

Lab ($\gamma=0.005$): fixed 0.743 overall, rolling 0.878, ACI 0.900; $\alpha_t$ dipped to 0.029.

A guarantee on the long-run miss rate, not on each interval.

Using all the data: cross-conformal and jackknife+

Split conformal wastes data: the calibration points never train the model. Fine with thousands of points, costly with 200.

Cross-conformal: $K$ folds; each fold is scored by the model trained on the other $K-1$. Every point is used for fitting and for scoring.

Jackknife+ (Barber et al., 2021): $n$ leave-one-out models; the interval combines $\hmu_{-i}(x)\pm|y_i-\hmu_{-i}(x_i)|$ over $i$. Guarantee $1-2\alpha$ in the worst case, about $1-\alpha$ in practice. CV+ is its $K$-fold version.

The price is computation ($K$ or $n$ fits) and a slightly weaker guarantee. Full conformal, refitting for every candidate $y$, is exact but rarely affordable.

Vocabulary of Extra 10

aleatoric
uncertainty from the noise of $Y$ given $X$.
epistemic
uncertainty from lack of data or knowledge of the model.
heteroscedastic
noise whose size depends on $x$.
prediction interval
contains a new observation with probability $1-\alpha$.
confidence interval
contains a fixed unknown quantity, e.g. $f(x)$.
pinball loss
$\rho_\tau(u)=\max(\tau u,(\tau-1)u)$; trains a $\tau$-quantile.
deep ensemble
$M$ networks from different seeds; disagreement = epistemic.
MC dropout
dropout kept on at test time, many passes.
ECE
weighted mean gap between confidence and accuracy.
temperature scaling
softmax$(\bz/T)$, $T$ fitted on validation NLL.
exchangeable
joint law unchanged by permutations.
nonconformity score
$s(x,y)$: how unusual $y$ is for $x$.
split conformal
scores on a calibration set, $\hq=s_{(k)}$, $C=\{y:s\le\hq\}$.
coverage

normalised score

CQR

prediction set
a set of classes with guaranteed coverage.
LAC

APS
